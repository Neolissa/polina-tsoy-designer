Bringing work, communication, learning, and recognition into one employee product.

Team
Designer, product owner, analysts, engineers
Constraint
Unify 5+ systems without interrupting work

Context

Five systems solved separate tasks but did not form one coherent experience

Employees moved between administrative tools, communication, a knowledge base, and development programs. Interviews, surveys, usability testing, and rapid checks narrowed the priorities to one entry point, mobile access, and a clearer recognition system.

Key insight: communication features could not replace everyday work. The platform had to preserve utility first, then strengthen cross-team connection.

Direction review: product decisions were discussed with employees, stakeholders, and engineers before detailed interface work.

Three decisions

Connect required work with motivating experiences

Each decision addresses a specific source of fragmentation.

01 · One entry point

Organize the product around employee tasks

Navigation and primary actions were restructured around what employees needed to accomplish rather than the boundaries between existing tools.

Before5+ separate tools
Afterone entry point

02 · Recognition and growth

Connect contribution to visible feedback

Recognition, achievements, development plans, and the knowledge base became parts of one system. Game mechanics were used only where action and reward could remain explicit.

  • Recognition: gratitude and visible contribution.
  • Growth: goals, resources, and mentoring.
  • Rules: a clear link between action and reward.

03 · System and delivery

Make the interface extensible and reduce implementation friction

Shared tokens, components, and templates replaced local rules. Monthly design-grooming sessions let designers and engineers resolve clear UI issues together and group larger changes before they entered the backlog.

Trade-off: legacy and new patterns coexisted during migration, but the team could keep shipping product work.

Reflection

What the product had to preserve

The hardest balance was avoiding a layer of entertainment on top of a required work tool. Administrative tasks stayed clear first; communication and recognition were added around that utility.

Joint sessions with engineers became an ongoing way to protect interface quality after launch rather than a one-off review ritual.
