## 2. Product thinking & process

### How do you approach a new problem?

> I clarify the job-to-be-done and constraints first — users, business goal, technical limits. Then lightweight research (interviews, corridor tests, analytics if available), map flows, propose 1–2 directions with tradeoffs, prototype, validate, and ship in slices with a success metric. I share early rather than presenting only the final design.

### How do you work with PMs and engineers?

> I treat design as a partner, not a handoff. I join sprint planning, write clear Jira context, defend decisions with user and business evidence, and stay through QA against mocks. I’m comfortable with HTML/CSS literacy so my designs are buildable. I prefer open critique over late surprises.

### How do you use research?

> Mix of qualitative (interviews, corridor tests) and quantitative (metrics, click maps, A/B when available). I build CJM and user flows when complexity warrants it. I don’t block shipping for perfect research — I use the lightest method that reduces risk.

### Design systems — what’s your experience?

> I’ve built and evolved UI kits and systems that teams actually use. On WakeupWarrior we reduced styles by ~90%, roughly 3× delivery speed, unlocked White Label, and covered ~80% of UI with a constructor. I care about tokens, components, and governance that speeds teams up without bureaucracy.

### How do you handle disagreement with a PM / stakeholder?

Use **DocsBird** (problem framing):

> On DocsBird, the brief framed registration as “collect all compliance fields upfront.” That produced a 7-step signup and treated activation as paperwork. I disagreed with the *problem framing*, not just the UI. The real job was time-to-value under regulatory constraints. I pushed progressive disclosure: keep required checks, don’t make activation equal “fill everything first.” We cut registration from 7 steps to 3, launched in 4 countries, and conversion rose +45%. Reflection: I’d map mandatory vs deferrable fields with legal/PM even earlier in writing.

### Tell me about a failure / mistake

> Early in systems work I sometimes shipped components without clear contribution rules, and teams forked styles anyway. Lesson: a system needs ownership rules and examples, not only Figma components. On later White Label work I paired guidelines with the kit so reuse was the default path.

### How do you prioritize?

> Impact × confidence × effort, under product goals. I cut scope for MVP, protect the critical path (activation, trust, money/compliance), and park polish that doesn’t move the metric. I make tradeoffs explicit with stakeholders.

### Metrics you care about

> Activation/conversion, engagement/session quality, task success, support load, and delivery speed when the bottleneck is design/system debt. I always ask: what will we measure after ship?
